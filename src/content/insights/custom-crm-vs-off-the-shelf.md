Most organizations should **not** build a CRM. Mainstream platforms such as HubSpot, Zoho, Pipedrive and Salesforce cover the standard lead-to-customer pipeline well, improve every month and cost less to start than any custom build.

A custom CRM workflow becomes the better option only when the way you sell, onboard or serve customers is itself unusual — and that difference matters to the business. This guide helps you tell which situation you are in.

## Start with the process, not the software

Before comparing tools, write down the workflow you actually run today:

1. **Stages.** What are the real steps between first contact and a paying or active customer? Name them as your team uses them.
2. **Owners.** Who is responsible at each stage, and who needs visibility?
3. **Records.** Which pieces of information must be captured, and where do they currently live?
4. **Triggers.** What causes a record to move forward: a signed document, a payment, a site visit, an approval?
5. **Reports.** Which three numbers does management look at every week?

If this list fits on one page and looks like a typical sales pipeline, an off-the-shelf CRM will almost certainly serve you.

## Signs an existing platform is the right fit

- Your stages are close to *lead → qualified → proposal → won/lost*.
- Most of the data you track is contact details, deal value and activity history.
- The main pain is discipline — people not updating records — rather than the tool's shape.
- You need to be live in weeks, not months.
- You do not have budget for ongoing maintenance of custom software.

In these cases, spend the money on configuration, data clean-up and training instead. A well-configured standard CRM beats a custom one that nobody maintains.

## Signs a custom workflow may be justified

- **Your process is the product.** Dispatch, enrolment, inspections, multi-party approvals or field operations rarely map cleanly to sales pipelines.
- **You are fighting the tool.** Custom fields, workarounds and spreadsheets have grown around the CRM until the CRM is only a contact list.
- **Licence costs scale badly.** Per-seat pricing for a large team that uses a small slice of features can exceed the cost of building and hosting a focused tool over three to five years.
- **Integration is the core requirement.** The CRM must sit tightly inside other internal systems — accounting, inventory, a customer portal — and the platform's APIs or plans make that impractical.
- **Data ownership and location matter.** You need full control over where customer data is stored and how it is processed.

One sign alone is rarely enough. Two or three together usually justify a proper discovery.

## The hidden costs of building

A custom build is not just the development invoice. Plan for:

- **Discovery and specification** — mapping roles, stages and data before any code.
- **Hosting, monitoring and backups** for the life of the system.
- **Security updates** to frameworks and dependencies.
- **Change requests** as your process evolves; the system must evolve with it.
- **Documentation and handover** so your organization is not dependent on one developer.

If nobody will own these after launch, choose a platform.

## A middle path: configure first, build the gap

Many teams get the best result by keeping a standard CRM for contacts and pipeline, then building a small custom application for the one workflow the platform handles badly. The two systems share data through the CRM's API. This keeps the custom surface small and the maintenance cost proportionate.

## Questions to ask any vendor — including us

- Can you show me the workflow in my own terminology before quoting?
- What would you recommend if we did *not* build custom software?
- Who owns the code, data and hosting accounts at the end?
- What does maintenance cost per month after launch, and what does it cover?
- How do we export our data if we leave?

If a vendor cannot answer these clearly, slow down.

## Summary

Default to an existing CRM. Consider a custom workflow when your process is genuinely different, your team is working around the tool, and you can commit to owning the software after launch. When in doubt, configure a platform first and build only the gap.
